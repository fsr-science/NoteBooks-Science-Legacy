## 1. Changing Trends & Career in Physical Education

### Definition

Physical Education is education *through* physical activities, aimed at the all-round development of personality — physical, mental, social, and emotional.

> 💡 **In your own words (from notes):** "A study about our physique which helps keep our health condition proper as well as creates career opportunities for the future is called physical education."

### Aim

All-round development of personality through physical activity.

### Objectives (remember as **P-M-S-E**)


| Type                      | What it improves                            |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **P**hysical development  | Strength, endurance, flexibility, posture   |
| **M**ental development    | Concentration, alertness, decision-making   |
| **S**ocial development    | Teamwork, cooperation, leadership           |
| **E**motional development | Confidence, stress reduction, sportsmanship |

### Development of P.Ed in India (Post-Independence)

1. Establishment of P.Ed institutions
2. P.Ed introduced as a subject in schools
3. Training programs — **B.P.Ed** and **M.P.Ed**
4. Organisation of national-level competitions and sports events

### Khelo India Programme

**Launched:** 2017–18

**Objectives:**

1. Promote sports culture among youth
2. Identify talented athletes at an early stage
3. Provide training & financial assistance to poor/deserving children
4. Improve sports infrastructure

**12 Components:**

1. Play field development
2. Community coaching development
3. State-level Khelo India centres
4. Annual sports competitions
5. Talent search and development
6. Utilisation, creation & upgradation of sports infrastructure
7. Support to national/regional/state sports academies
8. Physical fitness of school-going children
9. Sports for women
10. Promotion of sports among specially-abled persons
11. Sports for peace and development
12. Promotion of rural and indigenous/tribal games

### Fit India Movement

**Launched:** 29th August 2019

**Objectives:**

1. Encourage regular physical activity
2. Promote healthy lifestyle
3. Reduce lifestyle diseases
4. Organise fitness awareness activities

**Age Groups covered:** 5–18 yrs | 18–64 yrs | 65 yrs & above

**Logo:** Four Asiatic lions symbolising *Power, Courage, Confidence, Pride*. Below them: Ashok Chakra + a Bull (hard work, fastness) and a Horse (loyalty, speed, energy).

**Benefits:**

- Maximum number of people remain physically fit
- People can work extra hours
- Increases longevity of life
- Boosts physical health
- Reduces risk of mental and bone disorders

### Changing Trends in Sports


| Trend                         | Example / Effect                                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Playing surface**           | Synthetic tracks, astroturf, wooden courts → better performance, fewer injuries |
| **Wearable gear**             | Helmets, pads, gloves → protect players from injury                             |
| **Sports equipment**          | Lightweight, synthetic materials → better performance + durability              |
| **Technological advancement** | Hawk-eye, drone view, video replay, electronic timing → ensures fair judgement  |

### Career Options in Physical Education


| Career                 | Role                                         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| P.Ed Teacher           | Teaches sports & physical fitness in schools |
| Sports Coach           | Trains players, improves skill               |
| Fitness Trainer        | Designs exercise programs                    |

| Sports Journalist      | Reports sports news                          |

> 💡 **How P.Ed builds moral/character:** Through emotional development, social development, and discipline development.

## 2. Olympic Value Education

### Concept of Olympism

Olympism is a philosophy of life combining a balanced quality of body, will and mind.

**Principles:**

- Blending sport with culture and education
- Sport as harmonious development of humankind
- Concerted, organized, universal and permanent action
- Sport is a human right
- Sport as the framework of society
- No discrimination on the basis of colour, race, sex, language, religion, etc.

### Olympic Values (3)

- **Excellence** — giving your best on the field of play, or in personal/professional life
- **Friendship** — sport as a tool for mutual understanding among individuals
- **Respect** — for oneself, for rules/regulations, and for the environment

### Olympic Educational Values (5)

1. **Joy of Effort** — valuing hard work and determination
2. **Fair Play** — importance of sport in life
3. **Respect for Others** — respect for oneself, others, and the environment
4. **Pursuit of Excellence** — continuous improvement, personal best, commitment to excellence
5. **Balanced Life (between body, will and mind)** — maintaining balance between physical, mental and emotional well-being

> 💡 **Quick recall mnemonic:** **J-F-R-P-B** → Joy of effort, Fair play, Respect, Pursuit of excellence, Balanced life.

### Ancient Olympics (before 1896)

- First Olympic Games: **776 BC**, held during a religious festival honouring the Greek god **Zeus**, in the city of **Olympia**, Greece
- Held every 4th year, till the 12th century AD
- Games: Running, shot put, long jump, javelin, boxing, pankration, equestrian events

**Rules:**

- Only free-born Greeks (later Greek men) could participate
- Competitors needed at least 10 months of training prior to participation

### Modern Olympics (after 1896)

- **Pierre de Coubertin** (France) is credited as the founder/first president of the modern Olympic movement
- Governing body: **International Olympic Committee (IOC)**
- Founded: **23 June 1894** (some notes list 25 June — 23 June is the standard cited date)
- Headquarters: **Lausanne, Switzerland**

**Winter Olympics games include:** Bobsleigh, Curling, Ice hockey, Nordic skiing, Skating

### Olympic Symbol

Five interlocked rings of equal proportion, in 5 colours: **blue, yellow, black, green, red**.

- Colour order left to right: blue, yellow, black, green, red
- Blue, black, red placed at the top; yellow and green at the bottom
- The five rings symbolise the five parts (continents) of the world

### Olympic Flag

White field bearing the five equal interlocking rings (blue, yellow, black, green, red), with separation where two rings intersect.

### Motto and Maxim

- **Motto:** *"Faster, Higher, Stronger — Together"* (Latin: *Citius, Altius, Fortius – Communiter*) — recognises the unified power of sport and importance of solidarity
- **Maxim:** "Olympics is not only about winning but taking part. The essential thing in life is not conquering but fighting well."

### The Flame and Torch Relay

- The flame is an expression of positive values — it announces the Games and carries a message of peace and friendship; it also promotes the cultural and natural riches of the host region
- First flame lit: Stadium of Amsterdam, **1928**
- First torch relay: **Berlin, 1936**

### Olympic Awards


| Position      | Award        |
| --------------- | -------------- |
| 1st           | Gold medal   |
| 2nd           | Silver medal |
| 3rd           | Bronze medal |
| 4th, 5th, 6th | Certificate  |

### Olympic Movement Structure — Three Pillars


| Pillar  | Full form                       | Role                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **IOC** | International Olympic Committee | Formed 1894. Supreme authority of the Olympic movement. Holds the rights to Olympic symbols and other identifying elements. Main decisions include choosing host cities and electing new members.**~115 members** |
| **NOC** | National Olympic Committee      | Ensures representation of their country at the Olympic Games.**206 NOCs** currently                                                                                                                               |
| **IF**  | International Federation        | Manages and monitors the day-to-day running of each sport worldwide; responsible for technical aspects/scoring of their sport at the Olympics                                                                     |

**Other members:** OCOG, Athletes, officially recognised institutions

**OCOG** (Organising Committee for the Olympic Games): responsible for practical organisation of the Games — arrangements, training halls, venues, stadium, Olympic Village, and all organising infrastructure.

**Examples of International Federations:**

- **World Athletics** (formerly IAAF – International Association of Athletics Federations)
- **FIFA** – Fédération Internationale de Football Association
- **FIBA** – Fédération Internationale de Basketball
- **FIVB** – Fédération Internationale de Volleyball

**Criteria IFs must comply with:**

- Must have an anti-doping policy
- Must stage regular world and continental championships
- Must be committed to youth development
- Must have a robust and independent governance structure
- Must assume responsibility for control and direction of their sport at the Olympic Games

## 3. Yoga

### Definition & Importance

Yoga implies the unification of the physical, mental, intellectual and spiritual aspects of a human being. It is also considered a science that develops a person's consciousness.

**Importance of Yoga:**

1. Brings about physical purity
2. Prevents/cures diseases like arthritis, lower back pain, PCOS, etc.
3. Improves general health
4. Reduces mental tension
5. Reduces obesity
6. Gives relaxation
7. Maintains correct posture
8. Increases flexibility
9. Develops people spiritually
10. Improves moral and ethical values

### Ashtanga Yoga (8 Limbs) — by Sage Patanjali

Ancient Indian classical yoga, classified into 8 elements arranged in sequence from outer to inner self; the ultimate goal is union of the soul with the divine.


| # | Limb           | Meaning                                        |
| --- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 | **Yama**       | Moral restraints/principles                    |
| 2 | **Niyama**     | Observances (self-discipline)                  |
| 3 | **Asana**      | Postures                                       |
| 4 | **Pranayama**  | Regulation of breath                           |
| 5 | **Pratyahara** | Withdrawal of the senses                       |
| 6 | **Dharana**    | Concentration of mind                          |
| 7 | **Dhyana**     | Meditation                                     |
| 8 | **Samadhi**    | Absorption/ultimate union of the soul with God |

**I. Yama** — self-restraint from the struggle for survival; 5 moral conducts:

- Ahimsa (non-violence)
- Satya (truthfulness)
- Asteya (non-stealing)
- Brahmacharya (celibacy)
- Aparigraha (non-possessiveness)

**II. Niyama** — essential teachings for self-maintenance:

- Saucha (cleanliness/purity)
- Santosh (contentment)
- Tapa (discipline)
- Swadhyaya (introspection/study of self)
- Ishwar Pranidhana (prayer/surrender to God)

**III. Asana** — a set of physical postures that improve strength and balance.
**Types:** Corrective asanas, Relaxative asanas, Meditative asanas
**Benefits:** Beneficial for inner organs; reduces excess fat; regulates blood/oxygen flow; increases flexibility & agility by stretching muscles.

**IV. Pranayama** — controlled breathing; 3 stages: Puraka (inhale), Kumbhaka (retain), Rechaka (exhale)
**Types:** Ujjayi, Bahya, Digra, Anulom-Vilom, Bhastrika, Nadi Sodhana, Kapalbhati
**Benefits:** Good for lungs; boosts red blood cell count; improves heart & lung functioning.

**V. Pratyahara** — self-control of mind and senses.
**Benefits:** A self-control process where a person stops responding to external stimuli that disrupt mental concentration, and starts meditating inward.

**VI. Dharana** — concentration of the mind.

**VII. Dhyana** — meditation.

**VIII. Samadhi** — ultimate union of the individual soul with God.

**Overall Benefits of Yoga:**

- Improves flexibility, breathing, and balance
- Lowers blood pressure and relieves stress
- Increases stamina, bone density and muscle strength
- Boosts mental and spiritual well-being

### Yogic Kriya (Shatkarma / Suddhikriya)

The body needs both internal and external cleanliness. Inner cleanliness is achieved through 6 Yogic Kriyas:


| Kriya           | Function                                                         |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Neti**        | Nasal cleansing                                                  |
| **Dhauti**      | Cleaning of the digestive tract, especially oesophagus & stomach |
| **Nauli**       | Abdominal massage                                                |
| **Kapalabhati** | Purification of lungs and brain                                  |
| **Basti**       | Colon cleaning                                                   |
| **Trataka**     | Purification of the eyes, to overcome eye problems               |

**Benefits:**

1. Overcoming diseases
2. Creating harmony between body and mind
3. Purification of the body, leading to better energy flow
4. Improves capacity to work, think, and feel/experience

## 4. Physical Education & Sports for CWSN (Children with Special Needs)

### Adaptive Physical Education

A diversified program of developmental activities, games and sports suited to the interests, capacities and limitations of students with disabilities.

**Aim:** To ensure physical education for children with disabilities is delivered by a qualified Adapted Physical Educator.

**Needs for Adaptive PE:**

1. Children with disabilities have special needs because they can't take part in regular physical exercises/activities
2. Adaptive PE offers developmental activities/games/sports suited to their interests, capacities and limitations
3. Focuses on individualised programs for safe/successful participation in PE
4. Provides safe, personally satisfying, successful experiences of physical activity for differently-abled children
5. Focuses on development of composure, posture, balance, and coordinative motor skills

**Aims of Adaptive PE:**

1. Providing PE that meets the unique needs of children
2. Development of students' motor skills
3. Assisting differently-abled children in achieving mental, emotional, social & physical growth
4. Achieving meaningful success and positive educational outcomes
5. Developing self-esteem and improving socialization skills
6. Coping with disabilities through an active, healthy lifestyle
7. Developing cognitive abilities to improve educational results

**Objectives of Adaptive PE:**

1. Develop motor skills
2. Improve physical fitness
3. Ensure students' participation in PE programs
4. Promote sportsmanship qualities
5. Ensure services to differently-abled students
6. Improve social adjustment
7. Develop knowledge of good mechanisms

### Disability

Defined as a condition or function judged to be significantly impaired relative to the usual standard for an individual — a restriction or lack of ability to perform an activity in the manner (or within the range) considered normal for a human being.

**1. Intellectual Disability**
Characterised by significant limitation in both intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour. Originates before age 18; marked by low IQ scope and significant problems adapting to new situations.

- **Symptoms:** Very little interpersonal skills; not following rules/instructions; reduced capacity to learn/solve problems/reason; lack of practical skills (e.g. activities of daily living)

**2. Physical Disability**
Long-term loss or impairment of a body part that limits physical function; includes motor deficiency, sensory impairment, visual/hearing impairment, and limitation to mobility/stamina.

- **Symptoms:** Lack of full range of mobility; problems related to senses (sight, hearing, speech impairment); lack of motor skill/missed developmental milestones; lack of control over limbs/body parts

**Causes and Nature of Disability:**
Accident • Poverty • War • Nuclear accidents • Poor access to healthcare • Inherited disabilities • Illness • Malnutrition • Poison & pesticides • Lack of education • Medicines & vaccines (adverse effects) • Dangerous working conditions

### Concept of Disorder

A disorder is an ailment that disturbs a person's health; it hinders performance and diminishes efficiency.

**Changes due to disorder:**

1. Changes behaviour, thought and emotion, possibly causing significant distress to self or others
2. Person is unable to meet their own needs, or is at risk
3. Starts small but, if untreated, can grow serious and progress into a disability

**Types of Disorder:** Mental disorder, Neurological disorder, Hyperactivity disorder, Eating disorder, Addiction disorder, Attention disorder

**Prevention:** Regular exercise; Physical education (increases mental control)

### Disability Etiquette

A set of guidelines on how to approach people with mental/physical disabilities — treating them with respect and care, and helping bring them into normal life. (The Disability Rights Movement, ~1970, is said to have brought world attention to disabled people.)

**General Guidelines:**

- Always respect the dignity of a disabled person
- When introduced, it's appropriate to shake hands
- Never patronise a wheelchair user by patting them on the head/shoulder
- Respect the individuality of the person
- Talk to the person directly
- If you offer assistance, wait until the person responds
- Don't show extra attention/added care — it can feel like sympathy
- Respect the person's privacy
- When someone with a disability asks for assistance, respond graciously

**Specific Guidelines:**


| Condition                         | Guidelines                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Speech difficulties**           | Give attention to the person; allow extra conversation time; use short, simple, concrete sentences                                                                                     |
| **Hearing loss**                  | Get attention with a wave/shoulder tap; speak clearly & slowly without exaggerating lip movement; use sign language if familiar to both                                                |
| **Vision loss**                   | Announce yourself when entering/leaving a room; communicate written info orally; offer your arm if walking together; not everyone reads Braille — ask for their preferred alternative |
| **Cognitive/language impairment** | Use simple, short sentences; treat with respect & dignity; give extra time to respond; encourage rather than correct                                                                   |
| **Mobility impairment**           | Don't push/pull/touch their wheelchair or walking aids; place items within easy reach; step down or bend to their level when speaking                                                  |

### Role of Professionals for Children with Special Needs (CWSN)


| Professional               | Role                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Counsellor**             | Trusted person providing a safe, supportive environment; discusses the child's concerns/feelings; helps with academic, social, personal & career development; runs programs on stress/anger/conflict management and positive thinking; helps the child cope better with the disability                                                 |
| **Occupational Therapist** | Helps CWSN children develop fine and gross motor skills (eating, dressing, bathing, drinking); develops sensory & cognitive development; maximises the child's potential to take part in everyday activities; develops physical coordination (e.g. hand–eye coordination)                                                             |
| **Physical Therapist**     | Manages children with movement disorders; teaches how to balance movement while walking, rolling, sitting, crawling; encourages independence/mobility, building self-esteem; evaluates movement & function with reference to mobility, balance, posture, fatigue; uses techniques like massage, exercise, electrotherapy, hydrotherapy |
| **PE Teacher**             | Determines the ability of CWSN students and the procedures needed to support participation in sports/fitness; teaches physical activities that reduce stress, depression and anxiety                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Speech Therapist**       | Builds communication skills — words, sounds of speech, gestures, eye contact; teaches children to express themselves via oral communication, aiding emotional development and confidence                                                                                                                                              |
| **Special Educator**       | Works for overall development of CWSN children; improves social, emotional & behavioural functioning; encourages family/school staff to understand the child's needs                                                                                                                                                                   |
